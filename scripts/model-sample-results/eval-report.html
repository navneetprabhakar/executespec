Key findings

  • deepseek-v4-flash is the value standout of the entire field. 100/94/94 across bands at $0.004 / $0.016 / $0.11 per run — roughly 25× cheaper than the frontier baselines ($2.8–3.1 at HIGH). Its HIGH score (94) ties the best open model and beats every native baseline at HIGH (gpt 80, gemini 79).
  • HIGH complexity is where models separate. At LOW almost everything scores 100; at HIGH the field spreads from 94 (kimi, ds-flash) down to ~40 (glm-5.2, minimax-m2.7, deepseek-v4-pro).
  • Three models collapse at HIGH: deepseek-v4-pro (100→39, 3/6 tasks), glm-5.2 (100→46), minimax-m2.7 (94→41). Strong at low/medium, but they fail to carry multi-task HIGH specs to completion.
  • Two PROMPT-FIXABLE gaps (not capability): devstral-2 and qwen3-coder-next produce clean, building, reviewer-CLEAN, accuracy-100 code but skip tests → testCoverage≈10 → the no-coverage trust cap pins them at 75. Both are coding-specialised open models that under-follow the shared "tests-mandatory" directive. A per-model prompt variant should recover ~25 pts.
  • grok-4.20 is strong (100/100/87) but token-hungry — 12.9M input tokens / $3+ — a real efficiency outlier vs glm-5.2/gpt-5.3-codex which stay lean.

Recommendations

  • Promote deepseek-v4-flash as the default cheap-tier coding model. Best price/performance by a wide margin; HIGH-capable; native key (no gateway tax). Strong candidate for FREE/utility routing.
  • Per-model prompt tuning — targets locked: devstral-2 and qwen3-coder-next, reinforcing the test-writing directive. Data-backed (both are prompt-fixable, not capability-limited). Re-measure after.
  • Do not route HIGH-complexity work to deepseek-v4-pro / glm-5.2 / minimax-m2.7 — they collapse on multi-task specs despite strong low/medium scores.
  • Fix the cache-read telemetry gap so cost dashboards/estimates reflect true post-cache spend (presently overstated).
