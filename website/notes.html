Research notes

ExecuteSpec is an independent AI R&D lab studying one question: can a coding agent be made to prove its work, cheaply enough to use every day? This page is what we have actually measured, how we measured it, and what we still cannot answer.

Published results

We drive the production harness — the same classifier, planner, worker, and reviewer that run customer specs — through public benchmarks. Identical frozen harness for every model, official scorers, no excluded runs. Every Terminal-Bench trajectory below is public, including the failures.

Method, stated plainly. Terminal-Bench 2.1 runs used 89 tasks × 5 attempts under a fixed 1,500-second per-task ceiling — tighter than some tasks declare, so these are internal comparative numbers, not leaderboard submissions. “Tasks solved” counts tasks solved on at least one of five attempts; “per-attempt” is the mean across all trials. All Terminal-Bench trials and agent trajectories are public on Harbor Hub at the links above.

What these numbers are not. Benchmark results measure harness+model performance on benchmark tasks. They are not a guarantee of results on your codebase, and we do not present them as one. Across the 882 DeepSeek Terminal-Bench trials there were zero non-timeout transport errors — every failure was the model, the task, or the clock, not the harness.

What the lab is working on

These are open questions, not results. They are listed so you can see what we are betting on and hold us to it — none of them is a shipped claim, and long-form write-ups follow the evidence rather than preceding it.

Can verification be cheap enough to run on every task? Open

The harness spends real money proving work: builds, test executions, review passes. Cost per solved Terminal-Bench trial is currently around $0.05 on open-weight models. We want to know how far that floor drops before verification quality starts degrading with it.

Which failures does an agent hide from itself? Open

A suite that prints “all passed” while exiting non-zero, a green run that only proves the tree compiles, a reviewer vouching for a workspace another thread is still editing — each of these was a real defect in our own harness, found by distrusting our own green. We are cataloguing the class.

Does a plan gate change what humans approve? Open

Every run stops for a human before it spends on code. We can measure approval rates, edit rates, and what people reject — but not yet whether the gate makes reviewers more careful or merely trains them to click through it. That second outcome would make the gate theatre, and we would rather find out.

How much does harness discipline substitute for model scale? Open

Open-weight models on a disciplined harness are landing within range of far more expensive routes on our benchmarks. We do not yet know how much of that is the harness, how much is benchmark shape, and how much would survive contact with a large brownfield repository.
